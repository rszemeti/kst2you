About

Release: Loading...

KST2YOU is a project by Robin Szemeti, G1YFG. It aims to provide a web-based interface to the KST service, accessible on any modern browser, regardless of the device. If you have a feature request or comments, please contact me via my email as published on QRZ.com.

Beacon data from UK Microwave Group / Beaconspot.uk

Beacon data is current data kindly supplied by Beaconspot, a website run by the UK Microwave group. If you enjoy microwave radio or would like to contribute to the upkeep of our servers, beacon network or just receive our monthly newsletter, please consider a subscription (just £6 a year at the time of writing) — every little helps! BeaconSpot and Microwavers.org appreciate your support.

Aircraft data from 1090MHz.uk

Live ADS-B/MLAT aircraft data is provided through a dedicated feed generously supplied by Mike Tubby’s amateur-operated 1090MHz.uk aggregator. If you operate a suitable feeder and would like to support the service, please contact Mike through the 1090MHz.uk website.

Precipitation data from RainViewer

The optional precipitation radar overlay uses live radar data from RainViewer.

Privacy: by default, your settings and contest-mode log stay in your browser only. If you select "remember me", your login is stored locally in your browser. If you enable "Sync with remote storage" in Settings, selected preferences such as rotator settings, precise locator, locator autoresponder preference, and contest-mode log/session data are backed up remotely after a successful KST login so they can be reused across devices. Those backups are tied to your current KST credentials, so if the KST password later changes, a new remote backup set will be created. Remote backups are kept for up to 12 months after their last access, then removed the next time that backup set is used. You can also download or delete the current remote backup set from Settings. I do keep a log of callsigns that have used the service and any DXC cluster spots purely for statistics. I do not log your messages either sent or received. Since I started collecting numbers in late October 2023, there have been logins to the service and spots reported.

A basic write-up on the various functions and features may be found on my blog https://g1yfg.blogspot.com
